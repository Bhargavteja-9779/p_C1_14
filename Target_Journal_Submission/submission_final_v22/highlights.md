# Highlights

- Gut-microbiome classifiers set for a 10% false-positive rate missed it (outside 5–15%) in 70% of
  classifier–population pairs across 36 healthy groups, against about 15% expected by chance.
- A higher AUROC did not mean a more stable false-positive rate across populations.
- Two released gut-health indices, trained largely on the same studies, labelled most healthy people in their own East
  Asian training studies as non-healthy.
- Resetting the cut-off on 100 unlabelled local people cut the error by 62% within sites, but not across sites.
