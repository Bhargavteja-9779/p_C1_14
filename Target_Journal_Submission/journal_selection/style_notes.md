# Style notes - iMeta Letters (own words; no copied sentences)

- Title: 8-15 words; often a finding stated as a sentence ("X reveals Y", "X predicts Y") or "ToolName: what it does".
- Opening: "To the Editor," then 1-2 untitled paragraphs: the real-world/biological problem, what is missing,
  then a "Here, we ..." paragraph that states what was done and the headline result.
- Body: 2-4 ALL-CAPS headings, each a full finding sentence (or RESULTS AND DISCUSSION with declarative subheads).
  Each section points to Figure panels (Figure 1A-F) and Supporting Information (Figure S1, Table S2).
- Conclusion: short CONCLUSION section; limitations in 1-3 plain sentences; future use.
- METHODS: one line pointing to Supporting Information.
- Back matter order: AUTHOR CONTRIBUTIONS, ACKNOWLEDGMENTS, CONFLICT OF INTEREST STATEMENT, (FUNDING), ETHICS STATEMENT,
  (AI statement, used in 2026), DATA AVAILABILITY STATEMENT (GitHub + data source), ORCID, REFERENCES, SUPPORTING INFORMATION.
- References: numbered in square brackets, Wiley style; ~20 for Letters.
- Figures: 2 large multi-panel figures with a schematic panel (workflow) and result panels; vector graphics.
- Graphical abstract + ~100-word summary, plus "Highlights" only for research articles.
- Language: short declarative sentences, active voice ("we found", "we show"), numbers with CIs in text, little hype.

Our plan: write in plain English, short sentences, one idea per sentence, define each term once, intuition first.
