# Citation audit: manuscript.md (refs 1–20) and supporting_information.md

Audit date: 2026-11-23. Files audited were not edited.

How it was checked: Crossref metadata for every DOI (authors, year, title, journal, volume, issue, pages); DOI handle resolution (all 20 return HTTP 200); PubMed/OpenAlex abstracts; full text from PMC for Wirbel 2019 (PMC7984229, author manuscript), CRC-TrustBench 2026 (PMC13650428) and its correction (PMC13720279), Selby 2018 (PMC6433467), Van Calster 2023 (PMC9951847), Tong 2018 (PMC5804623), Roschewitz 2023 (PMC10587231), Thomas 2019 (PMC9533319), Mei 2024 (PMC11620793), Piccinno 2025 (PMC12283368), DEBIAS-M (PMC12087262); arXiv full text of Bates 2023 (arXiv:2104.08279); Nature supplementary PDF and **Source Data Fig. 3** spreadsheet for Wirbel 2019. Ozarda 2018 was checked from the PubMed abstract only (no full-text access).

## Summary

- **Metadata:** all 20 references are correct (authors, year, title, journal, volume, pages/article number). All DOIs resolve. No metadata corrections are strictly required. The optional points are listed in section 3.
- **Claim support:** most citations are appropriate. Problems are: [7] GLOBOCAN is used for a claim it does not support; [13]/[14] and [19] are paraphrased inaccurately; [8]/[9] give only weak support to "rarely reported"; [1] is cited for something it did, but the manuscript leaves out that Wirbel *did* report a held-out FPR (pooled, not per cohort).
- **Ordering:** [20] is cited in the text before [19]. Vancouver-style numbering needs a renumber (DEBIAS-M → 19, Roschewitz → 20), or the DEBIAS-M sentence needs moving.

## 2. Focus questions

**[1] Wirbel 2019: fixed FPR threshold on training data? Per-cohort external FPR?**
Yes on the first point. The cutoff "was adjusted to yield a false positive rate (FPR) of 0.1 on the controls of its respective (CRC) training set" (Methods). Main text: "our LOSO classification models calibrated to have a false-positive rate (FPR) of 0.1 on CRC datasets in fact maintained similarly low FPRs on other disease datasets ranging from 0.09 to 0.13 (Fig. 3c)."
No on the second point. Specificity or FPR per external CRC cohort is not reported. Source Data Fig. 3c gives a single **pooled** FPR on held-out CRC controls across the five LOSO folds (0.097). The Supplementary Information PDF has no per-study FPR table; its only specificity content is the FR-study ROC in Fig. S6 against FOBT. This is a useful point for the manuscript: a pooled held-out FPR near 0.10 can hide per-cohort drift in both directions, which is exactly what the manuscript finds. The manuscript should cite this explicitly instead of implying that no FPR was reported.

**[9] CRC-TrustBench: thresholds or specificity per cohort?**
It uses a fixed 0.5 probability threshold, reports specificity and sensitivity at that threshold under LOSO/LOCO (macro summaries in the main text, country-level outputs in the supplement), and reports per-country conformal coverage, which falls below nominal. It does not set an FPR-targeted threshold or check a promised specificity. It is weak support for "rarely reported" and strong related work on non-transfer of conformal guarantees across countries.

**[10] Ozarda 2018: "verify reference intervals with about 20 local samples"?** Supported (CLSI EP28-A3c, "minimum of 20 samples from healthy subjects from the local population"). This is a verification of a transferred interval, not a re-derivation (see nuance above).

**[17] / [18] order-statistic rules.** [18] supports the binomial P(FPR > α) ≤ δ rule exactly, including the n ≥ 29 and n ≥ 59 minimums. [17] supports E[FPR] ≤ α through marginal validity of conformal p-values, and explicitly says the guarantee is only an average over calibration sets. The ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉ rank is the standard equivalent but is not printed verbatim, so add a split-conformal primary or tutorial reference.

**[6] Selby / [7] GLOBOCAN.** [6] is appropriate: FIT positivity leads to colonoscopy, with threshold–colonoscopy trade-offs. [7] does not support the sentence. Also, the 0.5% screening prevalence used for harm calculations (main text, SI and Figure 3) has no citation. Selby's own cohort had 0.19% 2-year CRC incidence; Imperiale et al. 2014 (*N Engl J Med* 370:1287–1297, doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1311194) had about 0.65% (65/9,989). Cite a source, and state that 0.5% is within the range observed in average-risk screening populations.

**Is "Whether it still gives the promised FPR at a new hospital is rarely reported" defensible?**
Partly, and not as currently cited. Neither [8] nor [9] measures reporting frequency. The closest prior work did report realised FPR at a transferred threshold: Wirbel (pooled across held-out studies, and per non-CRC disease cohort), CRC-TrustBench (specificity at 0.5 per held-out domain, plus conformal coverage per country), and Roschewitz in imaging. A defensible version: "Realised FPR at a pre-fixed threshold is seldom reported per held-out cohort: Wirbel et al. reported it pooled across held-out studies (0.097) [1], and CRC-TrustBench used a fixed 0.5 cut-off rather than a target FPR [9]; in medical imaging, operating points drift even when AUROC transfers [19]." Otherwise, a systematic count would be needed to support "rarely".

## 3. Claims lacking a needed citation

1. "Microbiome diagnostics have no such step." This is an unreferenced assertion. Soften it ("to our knowledge, no microbiome reporting guideline requires…") and cite STORMS (Mirzayi C et al., *Nat Med* 2021;27:1885–1892, doi:10.1038/s41591-021-01552-x) as the relevant guideline.
2. "Likely reasons include DNA extraction, sequencing depth, diet, geography and how controls were defined." This needs citations, e.g. Costea et al. 2017 *Nat Biotechnol* (DNA extraction standardisation), Sinha et al. 2017 *Nat Biotechnol* (MBQC), and He et al. 2018 *Nat Med* (regional variation). Wirbel [1] also reports "study" as the dominant source of compositional variance.
3. CRC prevalence of 0.5% in screening (harm analysis): no source (see above).
4. "Journals and reporting guidelines for microbiome diagnostics could ask for step (i)": cite STORMS, and optionally STARD 2015.
5. "This rule comes from split-conformal prediction": add an originating or tutorial reference (Vovk, Gammerman & Shafer 2005; Lei et al. 2018 *JASA* 113:1094–1111; or Angelopoulos & Bates 2023 *Found Trends Mach Learn*).
6. Introduction: no prior work showing operating-point drift despite preserved AUROC is cited. Cite Roschewitz [19] (imaging) and Wirbel's single-study FPR spread (0.00–0.55) [1] there.

5. **[1] "normally" / "usual way":** soften to "as in [1]", or add further examples. Discuss Wirbel's pooled-FPR result as context: pooling hides two-directional per-cohort drift.
