# Highlights

- At a cut-off set for a 10% false-positive rate, 28 gut-microbiome classifiers for 14 diseases flagged 0–77% of healthy
  people across 36 general-population cohorts (6,525 adults, 20 countries); AUROC did not predict which cohorts failed.
- A released gut-health index (GMWI2) labelled 64% of healthy people in its own East Asian training studies as
  non-healthy, against 12% elsewhere; retraining traced this to the geographic make-up of the training data.
- Recalibrating the cut-off on 100 unlabelled local people cut the false-positive-rate error by 62% within sites in
  retrospective tests, but not across sites, at a cost in sensitivity; the atlas and procedures are released as fprcal.
