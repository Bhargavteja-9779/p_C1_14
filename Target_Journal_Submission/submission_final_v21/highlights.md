# Highlights

- At a cut-off set for a 10% false-positive rate, 28 gut-microbiome classifiers for 14 diseases missed that rate
  (outside 5–15%) in 70% of classifier–population pairs across 36 groups of healthy adults (6,525 people, 20
  countries), against about 15% expected by chance; AUROC did not predict how much the rate varied.
- Two released gut-health indices labelled a median of 64% (GMWI2) and 67% (GMHI) of healthy people in their own East
  Asian training studies as non-healthy, against 12% and 21% elsewhere (the two share most training studies); for
  GMWI2, retraining linked this to the geographic make-up of the training data, although country size and case mix
  could not be fully separated.
- Recalibrating the cut-off on 100 unlabelled local people cut the false-positive-rate error by 62% within sites in
  retrospective tests, but not across sites. In a post hoc check, a released cut-off also shifted with the profiling
  pipeline (MetaPhlAn2 vs 3).
