# Highlights

- At a cut-off set for a 10% false-positive rate, 28 gut-microbiome classifiers for 14 diseases flagged 0–87% of healthy
  people across general-population cohorts of at least 100 people (36 cohorts, 6,525 adults, 20 countries); AUROC did
  not predict how much the false-positive rate varied.
- Two released gut-health indices labelled most healthy people in their own East Asian training studies as non-healthy
  (GMWI2: median 64% vs 12% elsewhere; GMHI: 67% vs 21%, pre-specified replication); retraining linked this to the
  geographic make-up of the training data, and a cut-off also shifted with the profiling software.
- Recalibrating the cut-off on 100 unlabelled local people cut the false-positive-rate error by 62% within sites in
  retrospective tests, but not across sites, at a cost in sensitivity; the atlas and procedures are released as fprcal.
