# 2026-11-24 — OBSERVATION / PROBLEM (resume in a new account)
I picked up the project on a new account. The newest line is manuscript v3: "the false-positive rate of a gut-microbiome
test belongs to the population, not to the test". Round-2 review is MAJOR REVISION. The statistician found that my own
"exact law" validation was true by construction (random within-cohort splits are exchangeable by design), and that
duplicated people sit in two cohorts at once (DhakanDB/GuptaA, JieZ/QinJ, ThomasAM_2019_c/Yachida). One of those
duplicates makes control augmentation look like it "fixes" GuptaA (0.62 -> 0.03), which is leakage, not a fix.
The domain reviewer found that many "healthy controls" are hospital patients, pregnant mothers or people with metabolic
disease, and that the most extreme FPRs (56%, 87%) come from one hospital cohort (VincentC_2016).
What I believe now (written before re-running): the between-population heterogeneity is real (reviewer's I2 ~0.9 on a
strict subset), but my headline ranges are inflated, and my guarantee section over-claims. The honest test of the
burn-in rule is a split that is NOT random: calibrate on one batch/country/sub-study, evaluate on another.
